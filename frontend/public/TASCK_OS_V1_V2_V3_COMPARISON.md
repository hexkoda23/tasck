## 1. Executive Summary

TASCK OS V1, V2, and V3 are not three unrelated products. They are three stages in the evolution of the same operating system for The TASCK Agency.

In simple terms:

1. V1 proves the basic operating platform.
2. V2 adds intelligence, command-center thinking, and decision support.
3. V3 turns the system into a structured lifecycle engine built around Business Cases.

V1 is the classic multi-portal system of record. It shows that TASCK can manage brands, staff, creatives, super creatives, deals, projects, approvals, contracts, wallets, tasks, and admin oversight inside one role-aware platform.

V2 is the AI-native command layer. It shows how TASCK can use market signals, pipeline intelligence, deal simulation, ecosystem graphs, and live operational views to make better decisions before competitors do.

V3 is the workflow operating system. It formalizes TASCK's end-to-end delivery method into a Business Case lifecycle: Connect -> Frame -> Plan -> Deliver -> Closure. It makes stage gates, approvals, creator matching, contracts, invoices, deliverables, scope changes, final reports, and feedback part of one auditable process.

The relationship can be summarized as:

V1 = foundation and role-based operations  
V2 = intelligence and decision support  
V3 = structured execution and lifecycle governance

## 3. The Relationship Between The Versions

### 3.1 V1 Is The Foundation

V1 establishes TASCK OS as a structured platform rather than a set of scattered tools. It defines the first complete operating environment for the agency.

V1 answers:

1. Who are the users?
2. What portals do they need?
3. What workflows exist across deals, projects, approvals, contracts, payments, and admin?
4. How can TASCK present its business model to investors, brands, and partners?
5. What does a credible demo system look like for the African creator economy?

V1 is broad. It covers many user types and business surfaces. Its strength is that it makes the whole TASCK ecosystem visible.

### 3.2 V2 Builds On V1 By Adding Intelligence

V2 assumes that the structured operating data from V1 exists, then asks what TASCK can do with that data.

V2 answers:

1. Which signals matter right now?
2. Which deals deserve attention?
3. Which market movements could become opportunities?
4. Which relationship connections matter?
5. What happens if deal assumptions change?
6. How can TASCK operators see the system as a live command center?

V2 is narrower than V1 in implemented portal coverage, but deeper in intelligence. It is strongest in the TASCK Command portal. It introduces the idea that TASCK OS should not only store work, but also recommend, simulate, prioritize, and monitor.

### 3.3 V3 Builds On Both By Formalizing Execution

V3 takes the operational foundation of V1 and the intelligence ambition of V2, then turns them into a governed lifecycle.

V3 answers:

1. What is the canonical unit of work?
2. What stage is each opportunity in?
3. What artifact is required next?
4. What approval is blocking progress?
5. What fee, contract, deliverable, or report is missing?
6. How do paid and grant engagements move differently through the same operating system?

V3 is more structured than V1 and more execution-oriented than V2. Its major product breakthrough is the Business Case.

## 4. Version-By-Version Explanation

## 4.1 TASCK OS V1

### 4.1.1 What V1 Is

TASCK OS V1 is the classic multi-role operating platform for The TASCK Agency. It centralizes the agency's basic business workflows into one product. It is designed as a professional system of record that can be demonstrated to investors, brand partners, creative partners, and internal stakeholders.

V1 is built around five role-based portals:

1. TASCK Staff
2. Brand
3. Super Creative
4. Creative
5. Admin

The goal is to make the TASCK business model understandable and believable. A viewer should quickly understand that TASCK is not just an agency using spreadsheets. It has a platform for managing deals, projects, people, money, approvals, and operations.

### 4.1.2 What V1 Focuses On

V1 focuses on operational clarity.

Its main areas include:

1. Deal origination.
2. Project operations.
3. Brand campaign visibility.
4. Creative opportunity management.
5. Super Creative team coordination.
6. Deliverable approval.
7. Contract tracking.
8. Wallet and escrow-style flows.
9. Task management.
10. Dispute and admin oversight.
11. Seeded demo data for Nigerian and African creator economy use cases.

### 4.1.3 Why V1 Matters

V1 matters because it creates the first credible product frame for TASCK. Before V1, TASCK's operations could be imagined as manual work across email, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, contract files, and bank transfers. V1 shows that these workflows can become a product.

V1's value is breadth. It covers many roles and many parts of the business. It is useful when TASCK needs to explain the full ecosystem.

### 4.1.4 V1 Strengths

1. Clear multi-role portal structure.
2. Strong demo narrative for agency operations.
3. Familiar SaaS interaction patterns.
4. Good coverage across staff, brand, creative, super creative, and admin needs.
5. Supports money movement concepts through wallet and escrow-style flows.
6. Presents TASCK as a serious operating platform.
7. Makes the African creator economy context explicit through brands, creators, campaigns, and Naira-denominated flows.

### 4.1.5 V1 Limitations

1. It is not AI-native.
2. It does not deeply prioritize intelligence or prediction.
3. It uses broad role-based modules rather than one canonical lifecycle object.
4. It can show many workflows, but does not fully govern each opportunity through stage gates.
5. It is more of a system of record than a system of orchestration.
6. It does not make grant-funded work a first-class path in the same way V3 does.

### 4.1.6 Best Use Of V1

Use V1 when the goal is to show:

1. TASCK's overall business model.
2. How different stakeholders interact with TASCK.
3. How creator economy operations can be centralized.
4. A classic platform experience with multiple role portals.
5. A dependable system-of-record foundation.

## 4.2 TASCK OS V2

### 4.2.1 What V2 Is

TASCK OS V2 is the AI-native command-center evolution of TASCK OS. It is not simply a redesign of V1. It changes the product question.

V1 asks:

"Can TASCK manage the work in one system?"

V2 asks:

"Can TASCK see what matters, make better decisions, and act before competitors?"

V2 is organized around three portal concepts:

1. TASCK Command
2. Brand Portal
3. Talent Network

The current V2 implementation is strongest in TASCK Command. Brand Portal and Talent Network exist as routed concepts but are not as complete as the command experience.

### 4.2.2 What V2 Focuses On

V2 focuses on intelligence, prioritization, and decision support.

Its main areas include:

1. AI-style daily briefing.
2. Market signal intelligence.
3. Multi-view pipeline.
4. Deal room.
5. Scenario simulator.
6. Relationship ecosystem graph.
7. Live command screen.
8. Natural-language command bar.
9. Cmd/Ctrl+K command modal.
10. Operational pulse and mission-control style views.

### 4.2.3 Why V2 Matters

V2 matters because it shows that TASCK OS can become more than an operations database. It can become an intelligence layer.

Creator commerce moves quickly. Brand budgets shift. Campaign windows appear and disappear. Creators trend, become unavailable, or gain relevance. V2 is the version that says TASCK should not only track this movement but use it.

V2's value is ambition. It shows the future direction: prediction, recommendation, simulation, and real-time awareness.

### 4.2.4 V2 Strengths

1. Strong command-center identity.
2. Clear AI-native product direction.
3. Better executive demo impact than a normal dashboard.
4. Makes market signals part of the product.
5. Introduces decision support instead of passive reporting.
6. Includes deal simulation and expected-value thinking.
7. Uses network visualization to show brand and creator relationships.
8. Helps TASCK appear strategically advanced and data-driven.

### 4.2.5 V2 Limitations

1. Current implementation is strongest in TASCK Command and less complete in Brand Portal and Talent Network.
2. It is more focused on intelligence than full delivery execution.
3. It does not yet make one canonical operational object as central as V3's Business Case.
4. It can show what to focus on, but does not fully define every downstream stage gate.
5. Brand and talent experiences are planned rather than fully matured.
6. It is more demo-futuristic than production-workflow-complete.

### 4.2.6 Best Use Of V2

Use V2 when the goal is to show:

1. TASCK's future product ambition.
2. AI-native command and decision support.
3. Market intelligence.
4. Deal simulation.
5. Ecosystem graph thinking.
6. A more premium, futuristic, investor-facing product direction.

## 4.3 TASCK OS V3

### 4.3.1 What V3 Is

TASCK OS V3 is the Business Case operating system. It is the version where TASCK's actual delivery method becomes a structured product workflow.

V3 is built around one core primitive:

Business Case

A Business Case represents a full paid or grant opportunity from first signal to closeout. It contains the brand, creator, relationship manager, engagement track, qualification history, strategy artifacts, approvals, contracts, invoices, deliverables, scope changes, final reports, and feedback.

V3 supports three main portal experiences:

1. Admin Control Centre
2. Brand Portal
3. Creator Portal

### 4.3.2 What V3 Focuses On

V3 focuses on stage-gated execution.

Its lifecycle is:

Connect -> Frame -> Plan -> Deliver -> Closure

Each stage has a clear purpose:

1. Connect: qualify the opportunity.
2. Frame: align with the brand or funder and resolve scope risk.
3. Plan: select the creator, brief the creator, produce strategy, secure approval, and prepare contracts.
4. Deliver: manage deliverables, review work, handle scope changes, and execute the campaign.
5. Closure: generate final report, collect feedback, and preserve learnings.

### 4.3.3 Why V3 Matters

V3 matters because it turns TASCK OS into an actual operating method. It reduces ambiguity by forcing every opportunity into a known lifecycle with known gates.

V3's value is discipline. It does not only display information. It tells the team what stage the work is in, what artifact is missing, what approval is blocking progress, and what must happen next.

### 4.3.4 V3 Strengths

1. Business Case becomes the canonical unit of work.
2. Paid and grant tracks are both supported.
3. Stage gates make execution more auditable.
4. Brand Portal is more focused and useful than in V2.
5. Creator Portal is more concrete than V2's planned Talent Network.
6. AI is used in specific workflow moments rather than only as a broad product theme.
7. The system captures alignment, strategy, contracts, invoices, deliverables, scope changes, final reports, and feedback in one flow.
8. Demo seed data supports paid and grant narratives.
9. Frontend fallback behavior keeps demos resilient when the backend is unavailable.

### 4.3.5 V3 Limitations

1. The workflow is more complex than V1 and V2.
2. Some production integrations are still future work, including external e-signature, live messaging ingestion, real payment rails, and production AI integration.
3. Production authorization must be hardened before real external use.
4. The SDF gate needs consistent backend and frontend business-rule alignment.
5. The product is more structured, which may feel heavy for very small opportunities unless lightweight paths are added.

### 4.3.6 Best Use Of V3

Use V3 when the goal is to show:

1. How TASCK runs work end to end.
2. How opportunities become structured Business Cases.
3. How brand approvals, creator briefs, contracts, fees, deliverables, and final reports fit together.
4. How paid and grant engagements can share a common operating model.
5. A credible path from demo product to production operating system.

## 5. Detailed Comparison By Product Area

## 5.1 Product Philosophy

### V1 Philosophy

V1 believes the most important product problem is centralization. It takes workflows that would otherwise be scattered across many tools and places them into one role-aware platform.

The mental model is:

"Give each role a portal and make the business visible."

### V2 Philosophy

V2 believes the most important product problem is intelligence. It assumes the system has data and asks how that data can guide better action.

The mental model is:

"Help TASCK know what matters before everyone else."

### V3 Philosophy

V3 believes the most important product problem is governed execution. It assumes TASCK needs a repeatable delivery method and turns that method into software.

The mental model is:

"Every opportunity should move through a clear lifecycle with explicit artifacts and gates."

### Key Difference

V1 organizes the business by role.  
V2 organizes attention by signal and priority.  
V3 organizes execution by Business Case stage.

## 5.2 Portal Strategy

| Portal Area | V1 | V2 | V3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internal user portal | TASCK Staff and Admin | TASCK Command | Admin Control Centre |
| Brand portal | Full role portal concept | Planned/placeholder in current scope | Focused and functional client workspace |
| Creator portal | Creative and Super Creative portals | Talent Network planned/placeholder | Creator Portal with briefs, projects, deliverables, wallet, profile, messages |
| Admin/governance | Admin portal | Command controls and planned expansion | Admin modules for Business Cases, CRM, creators, contracts, templates, insights, reports, wallet, fees, tasks, users, settings |
| Portal entry | Role selector for v1 | V2 portal selector | V3 role selector |

### Explanation

V1 has the broadest role spread. It separates Staff, Brand, Super Creative, Creative, and Admin because it is trying to show the entire agency ecosystem.

V2 reduces the implemented portal depth and concentrates product energy into TASCK Command. Its Brand Portal and Talent Network concepts are important, but the current implementation is more about the internal intelligence cockpit.

V3 simplifies the portal model into three clearer experiences: Admin, Brand, and Creator. This is more production-friendly because it maps cleanly to the three main stakeholder groups in active campaign execution.

## 5.3 Core Data Model

| Data Model Question | V1 | V2 | V3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary records | Users, brands, deals, projects, opportunities, tasks, wallets, messages | Signals, pipeline items, deal scenarios, ecosystem nodes, command events | Business Cases, brands, creators, snapshots, briefs, contracts, invoices, deliverables, scope changes, reports |
| Canonical object | No single dominant lifecycle object | Deal or signal depending on page | Business Case |
| Data shape | Broad operating records | Intelligence and decision-support records | Lifecycle and artifact records |
| State management | Role dashboards and workflow data | Command-center state and simulated intelligence | Stage, gate, artifact, approval, finance, delivery, and closeout state |

### Explanation

V1 has many important objects because it is describing a full platform. The tradeoff is that no single object governs the complete business journey.

V2 introduces data that helps decision-making: market signals, scores, graph nodes, scenario assumptions, and live events. These are valuable, but they serve the command experience more than the full delivery lifecycle.

V3 chooses one dominant object: Business Case. This makes the operating model easier to govern because every artifact and action can attach to the same end-to-end record.

## 5.4 Workflow Model

### V1 Workflow Model

V1 is workflow-aware but module-based. Work is distributed across portals and pages:

1. Staff handle deals, pipeline, meetings, contracts, and tasks.
2. Brands review campaigns, approvals, spend, and talent.
3. Super Creatives manage projects and hire creatives.
4. Creatives manage opportunities, tasks, portfolios, and wallet.
5. Admin oversees governance, disputes, wallets, and platform health.

### V2 Workflow Model

V2 is decision-support-oriented. Its workflows are about:

1. Reviewing daily priorities.
2. Inspecting market signals.
3. Viewing pipeline in different modes.
4. Simulating deal outcomes.
5. Exploring the relationship graph.
6. Monitoring live operations.

### V3 Workflow Model

V3 is stage-gated. Its workflow is:

Connect -> Frame -> Plan -> Deliver -> Closure

Each stage has explicit artifacts and rules.

### Key Difference

V1 asks users to work inside role modules.  
V2 asks users to interpret signals and make smarter decisions.  
V3 tells users what stage each Business Case is in and what must happen next.

## 6. Direct Differences Between Each Pair

## 6.1 V1 vs V2

### Main Difference

V1 is a classic operating platform. V2 is an AI-native command center.

### What V2 Adds To V1

1. Daily briefing.
2. Market signal intelligence.
3. Pipeline prioritization.
4. Deal simulation.
5. Relationship ecosystem graph.
6. Live command view.
7. Command bar and command modal.
8. Stronger AI-native positioning.
9. More futuristic executive demo energy.

### What V1 Has That V2 Does Not Fully Replace

1. Broader role coverage.
2. More complete Brand Portal concept in the classic multi-role model.
3. Super Creative and Creative role distinction.
4. More complete full-ecosystem demo coverage.

## 11. Risks Across Versions

### 11.1 Risk: Version Confusion

Because all three versions are impressive in different ways, stakeholders may ask which one is the "real" product.

Recommended answer:

V3 is the strongest current operating model for execution. V1 explains the broader platform foundation. V2 explains the intelligence vision.

### 11.2 Risk: V2 Appears More Advanced Than V3

V2 may feel more futuristic because of its command-center style, even though V3 may be more operationally mature.

Recommended answer:

V2 is advanced in intelligence presentation. V3 is advanced in workflow governance. The final product should eventually bring V2 intelligence into V3 Business Case execution.

### 11.3 Risk: V1 Seems Obsolete

V1 may seem old because V2 and V3 have stronger concepts.

Recommended answer:

V1 is still useful because it explains the entire platform ecosystem and role model. It is the foundation, not a mistake.

### 11.4 Risk: V3 Feels More Complex

V3 introduces stage gates, artifacts, tracks, and more detailed operations.

Recommended answer:

The complexity exists because V3 models the real work more precisely. The product should continue improving UX so the process feels guided rather than heavy.
